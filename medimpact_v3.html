Assessment progress Step 1 of 6
Common HD presentations
Mild dysplasia

Shallow socket with minimal instability. Often causes groin ache after activity. May be manageable with physiotherapy for years.

Lateral CE angle: 20–25°
Moderate dysplasia

Significantly shallow socket causing pain with daily activities. Labral tears are common. PAO surgery is often considered.

Lateral CE angle: 15–20°
Severe dysplasia

Very shallow or absent socket coverage. Significant pain and instability. Usually requires surgery. May present with early arthritis.

Lateral CE angle: <15°
Diagnostic tests explained
X-ray (AP Pelvis)

The first-line imaging test. Measures the lateral center-edge angle and acetabular index to quantify dysplasia severity. Quick, inexpensive, widely available.

MRI scan

Best for assessing soft tissue — especially labral tears, cartilage damage, and fluid. Often ordered before surgery planning. No radiation.

Ultrasound

Primary tool for infants under 6 months. Safe, no radiation, and can detect DDH before the femoral head ossifies enough to show on X-ray.

CT scan

3D imaging used for complex surgical planning. Provides detailed bone anatomy including femoral version and acetabular orientation. Used less frequently due to radiation.

All treatment options at a glance
🦺
Pavlik harness / bracing
Infants · 0–6 months

Soft brace worn 23 hrs/day to hold hips in correct position. Success rate over 90% when started in the first 3 months. No surgery, no anaesthesia.

Non-surgical6–12 weeksHigh success rate
🏃
Physiotherapy
All ages · Mild–Moderate

Targeted strengthening of hip abductors, external rotators, and core reduces joint load and pain. Often the first-line treatment for adults. Best combined with activity modification.

Non-surgicalOngoingPain reduction
🔭
Hip arthroscopy
Teens · Adults · Labral tears

Minimally invasive keyhole surgery to repair labral tears and remove loose cartilage. Often performed alongside or before PAO. Recovery 3–6 months.

Minimally invasive3–6 months recovery
🦴
Periacetabular Osteotomy (PAO)
Adults 15–45 · Moderate–Severe

Gold-standard surgery for adults with healthy cartilage. The socket is repositioned to improve femoral head coverage. Preserves your own hip joint. 85–90% of patients report significant improvement.

Major surgery9–12 months recoveryJoint-preserving
⚙️
Total hip replacement (THR)
Adults 50+ · Advanced arthritis

Replacement of the entire hip joint with a prosthesis. Considered when arthritis is advanced and other options are exhausted. Modern implants last 20–25 years. Excellent pain relief.

Major surgery3–6 months recoveryLast resort
